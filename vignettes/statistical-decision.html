Introduction

Environmental monitoring datasets frequently contain censored observations, limited sample sizes, and varying analytical detection frequencies. These characteristics can substantially influence the validity of distribution fitting, trend analysis, and interval estimation procedures (Helsel 2012; Helsel et al. 2020). Statistical methods that perform well under low censoring may become unreliable when censoring is extensive.

The framework isinspired by recommendations commonly used in groundwater monitoring programs and EPA statistical guidance documents (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009, 1989; Interstate Technology and Regulatory Council 2013).

The manager package includes a statistical decision framework that automatically evaluates data quality and recommends appropriate statistical methods for:

  • Distribution estimation
  • Trend analysis
  • Censored-data handling
  • Prediction intervals
  • Confidence intervals
  • Tolerance intervals

Decision recommendations are based on:

  • Number of usable observations (n_used)
  • Number of detected observations (n_detect)
  • Percent censored (percent_censored)

The goal is to provide a repeatable and defensible framework for selecting analytical methods across environmental monitoring programs.

Censoring Classification

The degree of censoring is often the primary factor governing method selection for environmental datasets. As censoring increases, parametric distribution assumptions become more difficult to assess and trend analyses may require censored-data techniques such as ROS or censored Kendall methods (Helsel 2012).

The first stage of the decision framework is classification of censoring severity.

decisions <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = c(5, 25, 75, 95),
  n_used = c(20, 20, 20, 20),
  n_detect = c(19, 15, 5, 1)
  )

decisions |>
  select(
    percent_censored,
    n_used,
    n_detect,
    censoring_class,
    distribution_method,
    trend_method,
    trend_guidance,
    censored_method,
    interval_method,
    comparison_method
    )

Parametric Recommendation

Low-censoring datasets are evaluated using conventional distribution fitting.Parametric model selection is performed using EnvStats::distChoose(), which evaluates candidate probability distributions using goodness-of-fit procedures described in Millard (Millard and Kowarik 2024).

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 5,
  n_used = 15,
  n_detect = 14
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method,
    distribution_config
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 2
#>   distribution_method distribution_config
#>   <chr>               <list>             
#> 1 parametric          <named list [2]>

The default configuration evaluates:

  • Normal
  • Lognormal

distributions using EnvStats::distChoose().

ROS-Censored Recommendation

Moderately censored datasets are evaluated using censored distribution fitting. Regression on Order Statistics (ROS) is among the most widely used approaches for estimating distributions from moderately censored environmental datasets (Helsel 2012).

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 35,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 13
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 1
#>   distribution_method
#>   <chr>              
#> 1 ros_censored

This recommendation is implemented using:

EnvStats::distChooseCensored()

with left-censoring assumptions.

Nonparametric Recommendation

Highly censored datasets are classified as nonparametric. When censoring becomes extensive, distributional assumptions may no longer be adequately supported and nonparametric methods are generally preferred (Helsel 2012; United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 75,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 5
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 1
#>   distribution_method
#>   <chr>              
#> 1 nonparametric

Formal distribution fitting is not attempted.

Descriptive-Only Recommendation

Extremely censored datasets are limited to descriptive evaluation.

statistical_decision(

  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 1

Trend Recommendations

Trend evaluations are based on a Mann-Kendall framework, a widely used nonparametric approach for environmental time-series analysis (Helsel et al. 2020; Gilbert 1987). Mann-Kendall methods are particularly attractive for groundwater monitoring because they do not require the assumption of normally distributed concentrations and are robust to skewed environmental data (Gilbert 1987).

decision <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 25,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 15
)

decision |>
  select(
    trend_method,
    trend_guidance,
    censored_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 3
#>   trend_method trend_guidance    censored_method
#>   <chr>        <chr>             <chr>          
#> 1 mann_kendall formal_trend_test ros

Trend Guidance Categories

Trend recommendations use a Mann-Kendall framework whenever enough observations are available.

Formal Trend Testing

For datasets with at least five usable observations and low or moderate censoring, the framework returns:

trend_method = "mann_kendall"
trend_guidance = "formal_trend_test"

is recommended.

Screening-Only Evaluation

For high or extreme censoring, the Mann-Kendall framework is still selected, but interpretation is limited to screening-level evaluation::

trend_method = "mann_kendall"
trend_guidance = "screening_only"

is returned.

Insufficient Detects

When fewer than five usable observations are available:

trend_method = "insufficient_data"
trend_guidance = "insufficient_data"

is returned.

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 90,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 2
  ) |>
  select(
    n_used,
    n_detect,
    trend_method,
    trend_guidance, 
    comparison_method
    )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 5
#>   n_used n_detect trend_method trend_guidance comparison_method
#>    <dbl>    <dbl> <chr>        <chr>          <chr>            
#> 1     20        2 mann_kendall screening_only insufficient_data

Expected

trend_method       = "mann_kendall"
trend_guidance     = "screening_only"
comparison_method  = "insufficient_data"

The number of detected observations is used separately when selecting the recommended comparison method. It does not independently create an insufficient_detects trend category.

Deriving Decisions from Data

Use derive_decision() to calculate censoring metrics directly from a data frame.

For ordinary data frames, the default columns are:

  • analysis_result: numeric analytical result;
  • censored: logical censoring indicator.

A value of TRUE indicates a censored observation and FALSE indicates a detected observation.

gw_example <- data.frame(
  analysis_result = c(
    1.2, 0.8, 0.5, 0.3, 0.2,
    0.1, 0.4, 0.7, 1.1, 0.9
  ),
  censored = c(
    FALSE, FALSE, FALSE, TRUE, FALSE,
    FALSE, FALSE, FALSE, FALSE, FALSE
  )
)

decision <- derive_decision(gw_example)

decision |>
  select(
    n_used,
    n_detect,
    percent_censored,
    censoring_class,
    distribution_method,
    trend_method,
    trend_guidance
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 7
#>   n_used n_detect percent_censored censoring_class distribution_method
#>    <int>    <int>            <dbl> <chr>           <chr>              
#> 1     10        9               10 low_censoring   parametric         
#> # ℹ 2 more variables: trend_method <chr>, trend_guidance <chr>

This approach allows data characteristics rather than analyst preference to determine the recommended statistical workflow, improving reproducibility and consistency across monitoring programs (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

decision <- derive_decision(gw)

decision

derive_decision() calculates:

  • n_used
  • n_detect
  • percent_censored

and passes those values into statistical_decision(). Rows with missing analytical results or missing censoring indicators are excluded from these calculations.

Custom Column Names

Use result_col and censored_col when the data does not use the default column names.

custom_data <- data.frame(
  concentration = c(1.2, 0.8, 0.5, 0.3), 
  is_censored = c(FALSE, FALSE, TRUE, FALSE)
  )

derive_decision( 
  custom_data,
  result_col = "concentration",
  censored_col = "is_censored"
  )

The result column must be numeric and the censoring column must be logical. Missing required columns, invalid column types, and missing column names produce informative errors.

Grouped Data

derive_decision() supports grouped data frames and returns one decision for each group.

grouped_data <- data.frame(
  location = c("A", "A", "A", "B", "B", "B"),
  analysis_result = c(1.2, 0.8, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.4),
  censored = c(FALSE, FALSE, TRUE, TRUE, TRUE, FALSE)
  ) |>
  group_by(location)

grouped_decisions <- derive_decision(grouped_data)

grouped_decisions |>
  select(
    location,
    n_used,
    n_detect,
    percent_censored,
    censoring_class,
    distribution_method
    )
#> # A tibble: 2 × 6
#> # Groups:   location [2]
#>   location n_used n_detect percent_censored censoring_class  distribution_method
#>   <chr>     <int>    <int>            <dbl> <chr>            <chr>              
#> 1 A             3        2             33.3 moderate_censor… insufficient_data  
#> 2 B             3        1             66.7 high_censoring   insufficient_data

For env_schema objects, standardized analysis and censoring variables are resolved through analysis_result_var() and censored_var().

Distribution Estimation Workflow

estimate_distribution() uses the statistical decision framework to select a distribution-estimation pathway for each group.

The input must be grouped with dplyr::group_by().

Distribution estimation relies on methods implemented in the EnvStats package (Millard and Kowarik 2024), which contains many of the statistical procedures described in EPA groundwater monitoring guidance(United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
    ) |>
  estimate_distribution()

With the default distribution_config = “auto”:

  1. analytical columns are resolved;
  2. censoring metrics are calculated with derive_decision();
  3. a statistical decision is created;
  4. the recommended distribution pathway is selected; and
  5. results and decision metadata are returned.

Calling estimate_distribution() on an ungrouped data frame produces an error. Group the data before calling the function.

Automatic Method Selection

Under the default EPA profile:

Percent Censored Classification Recommendation
< 15% low_censoring parametric
>= 15% and < 50% moderate_censoring ros_censored
>= 50% and < 90% high_censoring nonparametric
>=90% extreme_censoring descriptive_only

A separate minimum-sample-size rule takes precedence: fewer than four usable observations results in insufficient_data.

Example:

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution()

The method-selection process occurs automatically.

Distribution Configuration

Each decision includes a recommended configuration object.

decision <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 10,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 18
)

decision$distribution_config[[1]]
#> $method
#> [1] "sf"
#> 
#> $choices
#> [1] "norm"  "lnorm"

Default settings:

list(
  method = "sf",
  choices = c(
    "norm",
    "lnorm"
  )
)

These settings are automatically supplied to:

EnvStats::distChoose()

or

EnvStats::distChooseCensored()

depending on the decision outcome.

Interval Configuration

The decision object includes interval-estimation guidance for methods supported by the selected distributional approach.

decision <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 10,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 18
  )

decision |>
  select(
    interval_method,
    interval_config
    )
#> <statistical_decision>
#> # A tibble: 1 × 2
#>   interval_method interval_config 
#>   <chr>           <list>          
#> 1 parametric      <named list [4]>

For parametric, ROS-censored, and nonparametric recommendations, the default configuration is:

list(
  conf_level = 0.95,
  method = "Bonferroni",
  k = 1L,
  n_mean = 1L
)

For insufficient-data and descriptive-only recommendations, the interval configuration is an empty list.

These values are configuration metadata intended for interval-estimation functions. The decision function does not execute interval estimation by itself.

Prediction Intervals

Prediction intervals are commonly used in groundwater monitoring to establish concentration thresholds expected to contain future observations with a specified degree of confidence (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Gilbert 1987).

Prediction intervals require a distribution classification.

A common workflow is:

dist_est <- gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution()

Standard prediction intervals:

pred_int(dist_est)

Upper prediction intervals:

pred_int(
  dist_est,
  pi_type = "upper",
  conf_level = 0.99
)

Simultaneous prediction intervals:

pred_int_sim(
  dist_est,
  pi_type = "upper",
  conf_level = 0.99
)

Incorporating statistical decision metadata directly into summary tables helps document methodological decisions and provides a transparent audit trail for regulatory and technical review (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Interstate Technology and Regulatory Council 2013).

    location_id,
    param_name
  ) |>
  stats_summary()

distribution_method
trend_method
trend_guidance
censored_method

This allows routine reports to include recommended statistical approaches directly within summary tables.

Example Workflow

A typical environmental monitoring workflow may look like:

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution() |>
  pred_int(
    pi_type = "upper",
    conf_level = 0.95
  )

Or for sitewide reporting:

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name
  ) |>
  stats_summary()

The decision framework automatically:

  • Evaluates data sufficiency
  • Quantifies censoring
  • Selects recommended methods
  • Configures distribution fitting
  • Recommends trend approaches
  • Provides interval defaults
  • Documents the decision basis

This approach promotes reproducible, transparent, and defensible statistical analyses for environmental monitoring programs.

Summary

The statistical decision framework provides a centralized mechanism for selecting analytical methods based on sample size, detection frequency, and censoring severity. The approach follows principles described in EPA groundwater monitoring guidance and modern environmental statistics references (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Helsel 2012; Helsel et al. 2020).

Key functions include:

  • guidance_profile()
  • guidance_options()
  • statistical_decision()
  • derive_decision()
  • analysis_plan()
  • validate_analysis_plan()
  • estimate_distribution()

Together, these functions support consistent and reproducible method selection across projects, analytes, locations, and monitoring programs.
