Introduction

Environmental monitoring datasets frequently contain censored observations, limited sample sizes, and varying analytical detection frequencies. These characteristics can substantially influence the validity of distribution fitting, trend analysis, and interval estimation procedures (Helsel 2012; Helsel et al. 2020). Statistical methods that perform well under low censoring may become unreliable when censoring is extensive.

The manager package includes a statistical decision framework that automatically evaluates data quality and recommends appropriate statistical methods for environmental monitoring data. The framework is inspired by recommendations commonly used in groundwater monitoring programs and EPA statistical guidance documents (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009, 1989; Interstate Technology and Regulatory Council 2013).

The manager package includes a statistical decision framework that automatically evaluates data quality and recommends appropriate statistical methods for:

  • Distribution estimation
  • Trend analysis
  • Censored-data handling
  • Prediction intervals
  • Confidence intervals
  • Tolerance intervals

Decision recommendations are based on:

  • Number of usable observations (n_used)
  • Number of detected observations (n_detect)
  • Percent censored (percent_censored)

The goal is to provide a repeatable and defensible framework for selecting analytical methods across environmental monitoring programs.

Guidance Profiles

Decision rules are controlled through predefined guidance profiles.

guidance_profile()
#> $low_threshold
#> [1] 15
#> 
#> $moderate_threshold
#> [1] 50
#> 
#> $high_threshold
#> [1] 90

The default profile is "epa_unified". The default profile reflects thresholds commonly encountered in EPA groundwater monitoring guidance and environmental censored-data applications (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Helsel 2012).

guidance_profile("epa_unified")
#> $low_threshold
#> [1] 15
#> 
#> $moderate_threshold
#> [1] 50
#> 
#> $high_threshold
#> [1] 90

Available profiles include:

#> $low_threshold
#> [1] 10
#> 
#> $moderate_threshold
#> [1] 25

These thresholds determine how censoring severity is classified and which statistical methods are recommended.

Statistical Decisions

A statistical decision object records method recommendations for a dataset.

decision <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 12,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 18
)

Censoring Classification

The degree of censoring is often the primary factor governing method selection for environmental datasets. As censoring increases, parametric distribution assumptions become more difficult to assess and trend analyses may require censored-data techniques such as ROS or censored Kendall methods (Helsel 2012).

The first stage of the decision framework is classification of censoring severity.

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = c(5, 25, 75, 95),
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = c(19, 15, 5, 1)
) |>
  select(
    percent_censored,
    censoring_class
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   percent_censored    censoring_class
#> 1                5      low_censoring
#> 2               25 moderate_censoring
#> 3               75     high_censoring
#> 4               95  extreme_censoring

Under the default EPA profile:

Censored (%) Classification
<15 low_censoring
15-50 moderate_censoring
50-90 high_censoring
>=90 extreme_censoring

Distribution Recommendations

Distribution estimation recommendations depend on both censoring and sample size.

Decision Matrix

Condition Recommendation
n_used < 4 insufficient_data
Low censoring parametric
Moderate censoring ros_censored
High censoring nonparametric
Extreme censoring descriptive_only

Example:

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = c(5, 25, 75, 95),
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = c(19, 15, 5, 1)
) |>
  select(
    percent_censored,
    distribution_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   percent_censored distribution_method
#> 1                5          parametric
#> 2               25        ros_censored
#> 3               75       nonparametric
#> 4               95    descriptive_only

The progression from parametric methods to censored-data methods and eventually nonparametric approaches is consistent with recommendations described by Helsel (Helsel 2012) and EPA groundwater guidance (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

Parametric Recommendation

Low-censoring datasets are evaluated using conventional distribution fitting.Parametric model selection is performed using EnvStats::distChoose(), which evaluates candidate probability distributions using goodness-of-fit procedures described in Millard (Millard and Kowarik 2024).

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 5,
  n_used = 15,
  n_detect = 14
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method,
    distribution_config
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   distribution_method distribution_config
#> 1          parametric     sf, norm, lnorm

The default configuration evaluates:

  • Normal
  • Lognormal

distributions using EnvStats::distChoose().

ROS-Censored Recommendation

Moderately censored datasets are evaluated using censored distribution fitting. Regression on Order Statistics (ROS) is among the most widely used approaches for estimating distributions from moderately censored environmental datasets (Helsel 2012).

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 35,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 13
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   distribution_method
#> 1        ros_censored

This recommendation is implemented using:

EnvStats::distChooseCensored()

with left-censoring assumptions.

Nonparametric Recommendation

Highly censored datasets are classified as nonparametric. When censoring becomes extensive, distributional assumptions may no longer be adequately supported and nonparametric methods are generally preferred (Helsel 2012; United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 75,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 5
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   distribution_method
#> 1       nonparametric

Formal distribution fitting is not attempted.

Descriptive-Only Recommendation

Extremely censored datasets are limited to descriptive evaluation.

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 95,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 1
) |>
  select(
    distribution_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   distribution_method
#> 1    descriptive_only

Trend Recommendations

Trend evaluations are based on a Mann-Kendall framework, a widely used nonparametric approach for environmental time-series analysis (Helsel et al. 2020; Gilbert 1987). Mann-Kendall methods are particularly attractive for groundwater monitoring because they do not require the assumption of normally distributed concentrations and are robust to skewed environmental data (Gilbert 1987).

decision <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 25,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 15
)

decision |>
  select(
    trend_method,
    trend_guidance,
    censored_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   trend_method    trend_guidance censored_method
#> 1 mann_kendall formal_trend_test          cenken

Trend Guidance Categories

Formal Trend Testing

When censoring remains below the high-censoring threshold and adequate detects are available:

formal_trend_test

is recommended.

Screening-Only Evaluation

When censoring becomes extensive:

screening_only

is returned.

Insufficient Detects

When fewer than four detects are available:

insufficient_detects

is returned.

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 90,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 2
) |>
  select(
    trend_guidance
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>         trend_guidance
#> 1 insufficient_detects

Censored Trend Methods

The decision framework also recommends how censored observations should be handled during trend evaluation.

statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = c(5, 40, 75),
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = c(19, 12, 5)
) |>
  select(
    percent_censored,
    censored_method
  )
#> <statistical_decision>
#>   percent_censored censored_method
#> 1                5     as_reported
#> 2               40          cenken
#> 3               75          cenken

For censored environmental datasets, ordinary Mann-Kendall analyses may not adequately account for reporting limits. In these situations, censored Kendall methods such as NADA2::cenken() are often recommended (Helsel 2012; Julian et al. 2024).

Recommended methods:

Condition Method
Low censoring as_reported
Moderate or greater censoring cenken

These recommendations are automatically used by functions such as:

trend_summary(
  gw,
  censored_method = "auto"
)

Deriving Decisions from Data

Most workflows will not create decisions manually.

Instead, recommendations are derived automatically from environmental data.

This approach allows data characteristics rather than analyst preference to determine the recommended statistical workflow, improving reproducibility and consistency across monitoring programs (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

decision <- derive_decision(gw)

decision

derive_decision() calculates:

  • n_used
  • n_detect
  • percent_censored

and passes those values into statistical_decision().

Example

gw |>
  filter(
    param_name == "Arsenic, dissolved"
  ) |>
  derive_decision()

Distribution Estimation Workflow

The primary consumer of statistical decision guidance is estimate_distribution(). Distribution estimation relies on methods implemented in the EnvStats package (Millard and Kowarik 2024), which contains many of the statistical procedures described in EPA groundwater monitoring guidance (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009).

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution()

For each group:

  1. Censoring is evaluated
  2. A statistical decision is derived
  3. The recommended workflow is selected
  4. Distribution results are returned along with decision metadata

Automatic Method Selection

Under the default EPA profile:

Percent Censored Recommendation Action
<15 parametric Fit candidate distributions
15-50 ros_censored Fit censored distributions
50-90 nonparametric Classify as nonparametric
>=90 descriptive_only Descriptive evaluation only

Example:

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution()

The method-selection process occurs automatically.

Distribution Configuration

Each decision includes a recommended configuration object.

decision <- statistical_decision(
  percent_censored = 10,
  n_used = 20,
  n_detect = 18
)

decision$distribution_config[[1]]
#> $method
#> [1] "sf"
#> 
#> $choices
#> [1] "norm"  "lnorm"

Default settings:

list(
  method = "sf",
  choices = c(
    "norm",
    "lnorm"
  )
)

These settings are automatically supplied to:

EnvStats::distChoose()

or

EnvStats::distChooseCensored()

depending on the decision outcome.

Interval Configuration

The decision object also includes interval-estimation guidance.

decision$interval_config[[1]]
#> $conf_level
#> [1] 0.95
#> 
#> $method
#> [1] "Bonferroni"
#> 
#> $k
#> [1] 1
#> 
#> $n_mean
#> [1] 1

Default settings:

list(
  conf_level = 0.95,
  method = "Bonferroni",
  k = 1,
  n_mean = 1
)

These values are intended as defaults for:

  • pred_int()
  • pred_int_sim()
  • conf_int()
  • tol_int()

Prediction Intervals

Prediction intervals are commonly used in groundwater monitoring to establish concentration thresholds expected to contain future observations with a specified degree of confidence (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Gilbert 1987).

Prediction intervals require a distribution classification.

A common workflow is:

dist_est <- gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution()

Standard prediction intervals:

pred_int(dist_est)

Upper prediction intervals:

pred_int(
  dist_est,
  pi_type = "upper",
  conf_level = 0.99
)

Simultaneous prediction intervals:

pred_int_sim(
  dist_est,
  pi_type = "upper",
  conf_level = 0.99
)

Summary Statistics with Decision Guidance

stats_summary() automatically integrates statistical decision outputs. Incorporating statistical decision metadata directly into summary tables helps document methodological decisions and provides a transparent audit trail for regulatory and technical review (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Interstate Technology and Regulatory Council 2013).

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name
  ) |>
  stats_summary()

Additional fields include:

censoring_class
distribution_method
trend_method
trend_guidance
censored_method
decision_basis

This allows routine reports to include recommended statistical approaches directly within summary tables.

Custom Guidance Thresholds

Organizations may prefer different censoring thresholds.

Global options can be used to override defaults.

options(
  manager.guidance = list(
    low_threshold = 10,
    moderate_threshold = 25,
    high_threshold = 75
  )
)

Retrieve the active settings:

guidance_options()
#> $low_threshold
#> [1] 10
#> 
#> $moderate_threshold
#> [1] 25
#> 
#> $high_threshold
#> [1] 75

Decisions derived afterward will use the updated thresholds.

Example Workflow

A typical environmental monitoring workflow may look like:

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name,
    default_unit
  ) |>
  estimate_distribution() |>
  pred_int(
    pi_type = "upper",
    conf_level = 0.95
  )

Or for sitewide reporting:

gw |>
  group_by(
    location_id,
    param_name
  ) |>
  stats_summary()

The decision framework automatically:

  • Evaluates data sufficiency
  • Quantifies censoring
  • Selects recommended methods
  • Configures distribution fitting
  • Recommends trend approaches
  • Provides interval defaults
  • Documents the decision basis

This approach promotes reproducible, transparent, and defensible statistical analyses for environmental monitoring programs.

Summary

The statistical decision framework provides a centralized mechanism for selecting analytical methods based on sample size, detection frequency, and censoring severity. The approach follows principles described in EPA groundwater monitoring guidance and modern environmental statistics references (United States Environmental Protection Agency 2009; Helsel 2012; Helsel et al. 2020).

Key functions include:

  • guidance_profile()
  • guidance_options()
  • statistical_decision()
  • derive_decision()
  • estimate_distribution()
  • stats_summary()

Together these functions allow environmental datasets to be evaluated using method-selection rules that are consistent across projects, analytes, and monitoring programs.
